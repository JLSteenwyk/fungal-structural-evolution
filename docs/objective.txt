  Develop and execute a reproducible comparative structural genomics project
  across approximately 500 fungal species spanning fungal diversity, plus
  approximately 25 non-fungal outgroup taxa.

  The central objective is to identify where protein structures change across
  the fungal phylogeny, determine how structural change relates to sequence
  evolution, and test associations with gene duplication, domain architecture,
  and ecological transitions.

  Proceed directly with the full sampling design; do not require a separate
  pilot study. Incorporate quality control, benchmarking, and intermediate
  checkpoints into the full workflow.

  PROJECT MANAGEMENT AND REPOSITORY

  Use a GitHub repository named fungal-structural-evolution to track the
  project, decisions, milestones, analyses, and reproducible scripts.

  Inspect the existing workspace and GitHub configuration first. Create the
  repository if account, ownership, and visibility are established; otherwise,
  prepare the local repository and ask only for the missing GitHub details.
  Do not overwrite existing work.

  Maintain:
  - A README describing objectives, scope, workflow, and project status.
  - A versioned sampling manifest and data dictionary.
  - A research plan with hypotheses, methods, and completion criteria.
  - An annotated bibliography.
  - A task tracker, decision log, and record of unresolved questions.
  - Versioned workflows, environments, configurations, and analysis scripts.

  Store large datasets and structures outside Git history. Track their
  locations, accession identifiers, checksums, provenance, and retrieval
  instructions in the repository.

  SAMPLING

  Select approximately 500 unique fungal species using both broad phylogenetic
  coverage and denser sampling around informative evolutionary transitions.
  Ensure representation of sparsely sampled fungal lineages.

  Include approximately 25 non-fungal outgroup taxa in addition to the 500
  fungal species. Select outgroups based on published phylogenetic evidence,
  emphasizing close relatives and using more distant taxa where justified.
  Do not require every outgroup in every protein-family analysis.

  For each taxon, document assembly and annotation accessions, source and
  version, quality metrics, taxonomy, ecological metadata, existing structural
  coverage, and the reason for inclusion.

  Evaluate assembly completeness, contamination, annotation quality, and
  redundancy. Record exceptions needed to preserve phylogenetic coverage.
  Represent uncertain or mixed ecological assignments explicitly.

  PHYLOGENETIC FRAMEWORK

  Construct a robust sequence-based species phylogeny and assess support,
  gene-tree discordance, and sensitivity to taxon and marker selection.

  Evaluate time calibration where defensible and propagate relevant dating
  uncertainty. Distinguish change per unit time from relative divergence when
  absolute times are insufficiently supported.

  Infer protein families, orthology, and domain boundaries. Reconcile gene
  trees with the species tree for analyses involving duplication and loss.
  Do not equate structural clusters with orthogroups.

  STRUCTURAL ATLAS

  Inventory suitable existing predictions and predict missing structures.
  Document prediction software, databases, settings, confidence metrics, and
  provenance. Evaluate systematic differences between prediction sources.

  Construct protein-level and domain-level structural comparisons and
  clusters. Identify remote homolog candidates, annotate supported functions,
  and characterize unannotated families.

  Distinguish structural similarity, inferred homology, and experimentally
  established function. Treat apparent structural novelty as a hypothesis
  requiring additional checks.

  EVOLUTIONARY ANALYSES

  1. Identify branches and clades with elevated structural change within
     homologous proteins and domains.

  2. Model sequence–structure coupling and identify excess or reduced
     structural change given sequence divergence.

  3. Analyze domain gains, losses, fusions, duplications, and rearrangements
     separately from changes within conserved domains and protein-family
     turnover.

  4. Test whether duplication is associated with structural divergence,
     asymmetry between duplicates, or changes at functional sites.

  5. Test associations with well-supported, independently replicated ecological
     or morphological transitions.

  6. Locate evolutionary changes in protein cores, surfaces, catalytic sites,
     binding pockets, and supported interaction interfaces.

  7. Where coding-sequence alignments and divergence permit, test selection
     hypotheses and map implicated residues onto structures.

  8. Reconstruct ancestral amino-acid sequences and predict structures for
     selected compelling families, propagating ancestral uncertainty.
     Prioritize experimentally testable functional hypotheses.

  STATISTICAL AND TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS

  Account for shared ancestry, branch duration, protein-family effects,
  uneven sampling, missing data, alignment coverage, protein length,
  prediction uncertainty, and multiple testing.

  Benchmark structural-alphabet branch estimates against direct structural
  comparisons. Do not interpret structural-alphabet branch lengths as physical
  displacement or assume raw structural distances are additive.

  Evaluate potential circularity because predicted structures derive from
  sequences. Check leading results using alternative prediction settings or
  methods and experimental structures where available.

  Do not interpret structural acceleration alone as positive selection.
  Separate inherited structural changes from prediction artifacts, domain
  orientation uncertainty, annotation errors, and alternative conformations.

  Make workflows restartable and scalable. Preserve species-specific variation
  needed for evolutionary analyses even when reducing redundancy for discovery.

  EXECUTION AND DELIVERABLES

  Begin with the repository, research plan, literature synthesis, sampling
  manifest, data inventory, and resource estimates.

  Estimate compute, storage, runtime, and costs before launching expensive
  stages. Use available authorized resources; obtain authorization before
  incurring new charges or provisioning paid infrastructure.

  Execute feasible analyses and produce:
  - A documented dataset and structural atlas.
  - Species and reconciled gene phylogenies.
  - Branch-specific sequence and structural change estimates with uncertainty.
  - Tests of ecological transitions, duplication, and domain evolution.
  - Reproducible figures, tables, and a methods draft.
  - Prioritized mechanistic case studies and proposed validation experiments.
  - Clear documentation of limitations and outstanding work.

  Track progress against explicit milestones. Do not present plans as completed
  analyses or mark the overall project complete while required work remains.
  When external resources or experimental work are necessary, document the
  dependency and continue independent tasks that can proceed.

  This sets the target at approximately 525 taxa total and keeps the phylogenetic integration central to the project.
